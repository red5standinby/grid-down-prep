Frame it correctly

A 2-week outage is a Tuesday emergency. Two months is a different problem.

What preparedness solves
  • Storms, grid failure, ice, boil-water notices
  • Store shortages / price spikes on staples
  • A few weeks of no resupply
  • Peace of mind + less panic buying
What it does NOT solve
  • Multi-month economic collapse
  • Long-term relocation questions
  • Anything a solo household can't do alone
  • Community/travel/logistics — those are separate

The sane target

2–4 weeks of water, food, heat, light, cash — sized to your household, rotated so it stays usable. Around a thousand dollars for a family, not several thousand.

Priority Stack

Ranked by ROI

1 · Water — highest ROI, full stop

Nobody dies of starvation in a 2-week outage. People get sick from bad water. 1 gal/person/day. For 4 people that's 28 gal/week.

2 · Heat & light without the grid

Propane + indoor-safe heater + CO detector. This keeps you *alive and functional* in winter.

3 · Food you already eat

Shelved versions of your normal diet. Rotate: eat oldest, replace. No weird food you won't touch.

4 · Comms, power, cash, meds

Solar + battery, weather radio, small bills, first aid, prescription stockpile.

Skip · Gold, ammo, tactical gear

Least liquid in a real disruption, easiest to overpay, and none of it feeds you.

  • Water testing kit — test drums before use, every rotation
  • PUR / Aquatabs as backup
  • Filter handles thousands of gallons

Rule of thumb: 1 gal/person/day drinking + 0.5 gal for hygiene. Double it if you have pets or it's summer.

2 · Heat, Light & Cooking

~$340
Heat
  • 3 × 20-lb propane tanks
  • Mr. Heater indoor-safe unit
  • CO detector — NON-NEGOTIABLE indoors
  • Wool blankets, sleeping bags
Cook
  • Camp stove + fuel canisters
  • Or propane grill you already own
  • Manual can opener (obviously)
  • Matches + 2 lighters + ferro rod
Light
  • 6 × LED headlamps
  • Rechargeable AA/AAA + charger
  • 3 lanterns (battery)
  • Never candles for lighting

Safety first: Anything that burns indoors needs ventilation + a CO alarm. Generators stay outside, 20+ ft from windows.

What it runs
  • Phones, radios, LED lights, laptop
  • CPAP / small medical devices
  • Small fridge or freezer cycling on/off
  • ~1 kWh/day harvest in decent sun
Why it's separate
  • Charges with zero grid — all-day solar
  • Swap/upgrade parts individually
  • Redundancy: two independent power paths
  • Battery is the expensive half — buy once

Safety: 12V + lithium still needs correct fusing, cable sizing, and a BMS. If wiring is new to you, buy a kit or get it checked — don't freestyle 100Ah LFP.

Skip List

Where money goes to die
Skip
  • Gold / silver coins as a "grid-down hedge"
  • Bulk ammunition
  • Tactical / camo gear
  • MRE cases (pay 5× for mediocre food)
  • Gas masks & "bug-out" cosplay
Do instead
  • Emergency fund in cash
  • Pay down high-interest debt
  • Normal inflation-resistant portfolio
  • Rotate your staple pantry monthly
  • Know your neighbors — real resilience

These items are the least liquid in a real disruption, the easiest to overpay for while scared, and not one of them feeds you.

Action plan

Do it in 4 steps, not one panicked weekend

Week 1

Water drums (55-gal) + gravity filter — 28 gal stored
CO detector + one propane tank & heater

2 weeks of staples + mylar/O₂ absorbers

Week 3

Second propane tank + camp stove
First aid + prescription stockpile

Ongoing

Rotate pantry monthly (eat oldest)
Solar + battery bank when budget allows
The goal isn't a bunker. It's a boring Tuesday you barely notice.
