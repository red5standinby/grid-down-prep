You're walking distance from the Housatonic.
That changes the plan.

The 2–4 week pantry plan still holds. But living inside the river's reach adds a second, sharper axis: water that arrives with a warning and leaves a mess. This addendum separates the two.

🌊 Flood axis

Localized. Gives hours of warning. Threat is elevation and access, not distance.

🥫 Grid-down axis

Regional. No warning. Threat is duration and resupply — the original deck.

⚠️ The trap

The two overlap in the worst events (hurricane → multi-day outage). Plan for the overlap, not either alone.

First, the honest read

The river is the best water source you have — and the biggest variable

Two things are true at once, and neither cancels the other.

What proximity gives you

  • Unlimited raw water — a 55-gal drum isn't your ceiling, the river is (with treatment).
  • A natural firebreak of low ground between you and main-road chaos if your block is on the high side.
  • Wild food — fishing/foraging is a real supplement, and a morale one.

What proximity costs you

  • Evacuation risk, not starvation risk. The river rarely starves anyone; it relocates them.
  • Access loss — low bridges and river roads flood first and dry last.
  • Contaminated water — floodwater is sewage + fuel + farm runoff. Treat it like hazmat.
  • Insurance + sellability drag in the floodplain (outside prep, but real).

How to fill this in (30 minutes)

  • FEMA flood map service (msc.fema.gov) → your address → look at the zone code.
  • USGS StreamStats or a topo map for spot elevations.
  • Walk the two candidate exits and note the elevation at the pinch points.

How to read the delta

  • ≥ +30 ft  You're above the realistic flood envelope. Bug-in is the plan.
  • +10 to +30 ft  Gray zone. Pre-stage go-bags; decide on the NWS forecast, not the vibe.
  • ≤ +10 ft  You're in it. Evacuate early; the pantry stays behind.
FEMA zone codes: AE/A (1% annual = "100-yr"), X-shaded (0.2%), X (minimal). Zones are a screening tool — not a promise about your exact lot. Sheet 3 of 7.
Tiered response

Four tiers, one trigger each

Don't decide under stress. Decide now, and let the trigger decide for you.

TriggerWhat it meansWhat you do
Tier 0 Watch NWS flood watch / river forecast rising Freeze the plan. Charge everything, top off water drums and vehicle fuel, move the go-bag to the door, screenshot the NWS hydrograph.
Tier 1 Stage Flood warning, or forecast crest above your delta Vehicle loaded and parked pointing at high ground. Upstairs: valuables, documents, meds, one week of clothes. Prescriptions re-filled now. Check on the two neighbors who are worse off than you.
Tier 2 Move Evacuation order, or water on your access route Leave on your own terms, not the county's. Cards work for gas before the region goes dark. Take the high-ground route, not the short one. Go to the rally point (sheet 5).
Tier 3 Out Water at or above the main floor, or 48h+ of no power and no clean water Bug-out. The default for a "river at the door" event. The 4-week pantry is not a reason to stay — it keeps working for the neighbors or for when you return.
Rule: the river floods fast and recedes slow. Being wrong on the early side costs an uncomfortable night; being wrong on the late side costs the plan. Sheet 4 of 7.

The bottom line

Which plan wins?

🌊 For the river: BUG OUT

A river flood is a localized, forecastable event with hours of warning. The correct play is early, uphill, and boring: load the car, drive the route you rehearsed, be somewhere dry before the peak. You are not defending a position against water.

🥫 For the grid: BUG IN

A grid-down event is regional and open-ended. Your house is the shelter, the pantry is the supply, and leaving means abandoning both into a system with no resupply. Stay — unless the river axis fires at the same time.

⚠️ The overlap is the real scenario

A hurricane or a multi-day ice storm hits both axes: no power for a week and flood conditions. For that one, the answer is bug out early — because you lose the grid plan anyway the moment water reaches your access route. Decide the overlap case now; it's the one that will actually happen.
